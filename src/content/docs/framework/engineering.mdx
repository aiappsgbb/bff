## Building the factory is a lit job

The engineering loop is part of the HQ: it never goes dark. A platform engineering team, or a partner, treats the factory as a product. Its customers are the people and agents on the line.

## Three moves

**Tune trust and control.** SLOs, approvals and policies. Decide who or what can approve each kind of change, under which identity, within which budget. At low autonomy this means rulesets and CODEOWNERS. At high autonomy it means policy as code and approval by exception.

**Expand automation.** Workflows, triggers and environments. Give agents somewhere safe to work and something to start them. At low autonomy this is CI/CD and golden dev environments. At high autonomy it's fleets per work type, end-to-end test environments and progressive delivery.

**Strengthen foundations.** Skills, tools and context. Every incident, review and eval is a chance to make the next session better. At low autonomy this means starter instructions and approved MCP servers. At high autonomy, agents curate skills and memory, and people review every update.

## One loop, every level

The loop doesn't change between levels. What changes is the size of each move. Use the tabs above to see the engineering work for each level, or open the [playbook](/playbooks/) for step-by-step instructions.

## Know where to improve

The HQ measures the factory with DORA and SPACE, per service and work type. The weakest signal picks the next move: slow reviews point to trust and control, waits in CI or deployment point to automation, and rework points to foundations. [Measuring progress →](/framework/measuring/)
