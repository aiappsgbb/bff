## Every team already runs a factory

A software factory is the system that turns ideas into running software: people, process, tools and platform working as one line. Ideas go in, running software comes out, and what you learn in production comes back in as new ideas.

Every line has the same four stations. **Plan** turns intent into specs. **Build** turns specs into code. **Verify** turns code into evidence. **Observe** turns production signals into new work.

Most teams already have this line, spread across trackers, chats, pipelines and scripts. The question is not whether you have a factory. It's how much of it you designed.

</ScrollyStep>
<ScrollyStep frame="agents">

## Agents change the economics

Agents can now plan, write, review, test and fix code. They work in parallel, around the clock, and never tire of the jobs people put off.

The line can carry many changes at once. The bottleneck moves from writing code to **deciding what to build** and **checking what was built**. A factory that ignores this gets more code, not more value.

</ScrollyStep>

## The dark factory trap

In manufacturing, a *dark factory* runs with the lights off: fully automated, nobody on the floor. **Robots don't need light to work. People do.**

Applied to software, the dark factory is tempting on paper and risky in practice: decisions nobody can explain, risks nobody sees, and no way back when something goes wrong. Lights out at once means humans out of the loop.

</ScrollyStep>
<ScrollyStep frame="bright">

## The bright factory

The Bright Factory Framework takes the opposite view. **Ideas come to life. Agents do the heavy lifting. Humans monitor and control.**

The factory floor, where agents work, can dim. The **Factory HQ**, where people work, stays bright: ideas, creativity, building the factory, and monitoring and control. Every action on the floor is traced and visible from the HQ.

**The floor can go dark. The factory never does.**

</ScrollyStep>
<ScrollyStep frame="dimmer">

## A dimmer, not a switch

Automation isn't on or off. It's set per work type, on a dimmer with three positions:

- **Crawl**, low automation: lights on. Humans drive every step, agents assist.
- **Walk**, medium automation: dimmed. Agents do the work, people approve.
- **Run**, high automation: lights-off. The floor runs on its own, the HQ stays bright.

Each work type moves up when the evidence says so, and can dim back up in minutes when signals say otherwise.

</ScrollyStep>
